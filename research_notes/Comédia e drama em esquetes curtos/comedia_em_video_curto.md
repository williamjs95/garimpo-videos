# Comedy in short vertical video (TikTok / Reels / Shorts), 2025-2026: structure, retention, animated series and brand comedy

Research date: 2026-11-21. Reading guide: each finding is labelled **[OFFICIAL]** (platform statement, directly or as reported by trade press), **[STUDY]** (third-party dataset, method often not public), **[PRACTITIONER]** (interview / craft advice) or **[FOLKLORE]** (widely repeated, no primary source found). Many numbers circulating about hooks and completion are folklore; they are flagged, not endorsed.

---

## 1. What drives distribution in 2025-2026 (official guidance and benchmarks)

### Takeaway
The only hard official signals are: Instagram = watch time, likes per reach, sends per reach (sends matter most for reaching non-followers); YouTube Shorts = whether people choose to watch instead of swiping, with no public threshold; TikTok publishes no hook or completion benchmark. Every "70% 3-second retention" type number is vendor or creator data, so the series should benchmark against its own account medians.

### Cited Findings

**Instagram**
- [OFFICIAL] Adam Mosseri, first video of his ranking series (reported Jan 22, 2025): "The top three signals that matter most for ranking are watch time, likes and sends." and "pay close attention to average watch time, likes per reach, and sends per reach." — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-insights-2025/738034/)
- [OFFICIAL] Same video: "Likes are slightly more important for connected content, and sends are slightly more important for unconnected content." (connected = followers; unconnected = non-followers / recommendations) — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-insights-2025/738034/)
- [OFFICIAL, secondhand] Comments are not among the three named signals; the article's author (not Mosseri) speculates they feed into watch time — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-insights-2025/738034/)
- [OFFICIAL, via Buffer] Mosseri (part four of the series) says creators look too much at raw views/likes; rates matter more than counts. Instagram Insights labels sends as "shares", so sends per reach = shares / accounts reached — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/instagram-algorithms/)
- [OFFICIAL, via Buffer] Originality: "We want to create a platform that gives more distribution to the original content creator than to those who repost it"; Reels carrying other apps' logos/watermarks are pushed down; aggregator accounts lose recommendation to non-followers (Buffer dates the latter to April 2026) — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/instagram-algorithms/)
- [OFFICIAL, via Buffer] Mosseri on the Colin and Samir podcast (undated in the article): about half of Instagram videos are watched without sound — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/instagram-algorithms/)
- [OFFICIAL, secondhand] Length: at a New York creator event Instagram showed a slide saying Reels longer than 90 seconds can hurt distribution; in January 2025 Mosseri announced the limit going to 3 minutes, saying "90 seconds is just too short" — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-longer-reels-hurt-performance/715867/), [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-will-recommend-longer-reels/737913/)
- [STUDY] Buffer, 2.7 million Reels (Jan-Aug 2026): 90-179 s Reels had about 7.4% more reach than each account's baseline; 30-59 s Reels about 2.7% more. Reels get about 36% more reach than carousels; carousels about 12% more engagement (4M+ posts). Methodology not visible — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/instagram-reels-length), [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/instagram-algorithms/)
- [FOLKLORE] A claim that sends per reach is weighted "three to five times" a like appears on secondary blogs with no primary source; Mosseri has published no numeric weights — surfaced in search of [Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/instagram-sends-per-reach-playbook/) and similar; treat as unverified.
- No official Instagram statement on a "3-second hold" metric was found; the "3-second rule" in Buffer's guide is Buffer's own advice — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/instagram-algorithms/)

**YouTube Shorts**
- [OFFICIAL, via vidIQ] Since March 31, 2025, "any YouTube Short that starts playing or replays will be counted as a view, with no minimum watch time required"; the older stricter metric is now "Engaged views", and only engaged views count for Partner Program eligibility and revenue. Every loop adds a view — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-shorts-algorithm/)
- [OFFICIAL, paraphrased] Todd Sherman (Shorts product lead), Creator Insider interview: most Shorts views come from the feed, not deliberate choice, so the system tests each Short on a small audience; YouTube does not share thresholds to avoid gaming — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-shorts-algorithm/), [Search Engine Journal](https://searchenginejournal.com/youtube-explains-how-shorts-algorithm-works/494953) (2023), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/08/25/youtube-demystifies-the-shorts-algorithm-views-and-answers-other-creator-questions/) (Aug 2023)
- [OFFICIAL feature] "Viewed vs. swiped away" is a YouTube Studio metric (Analytics > Content > Shorts); no official benchmark exists. One creator's 90-day example was 26.6% viewed / 73.4% swiped; guides advise building a channel-specific baseline — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-shorts-algorithm/), [ContentStudio](https://contentstudio.io/blog/youtube-shorts-analytics-guide)
- [OFFICIAL feature] Creators can attach a related video to a Short (useful for linking episodes) — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-shorts-algorithm/)
- [STUDY] Nate Black, 35 billion Shorts views: Shorts of 13 or 60 seconds performed best; channels with 200+ Shorts saw consistent growth; first three seconds crucial (no percentage) — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-shorts-algorithm/)
- [STUDY, 2023] Paddy Galloway: "Just making a long short isn't the goal, making a short that holds people for as long as possible is." — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/youtube-shorts-algorithm/)
- [STUDY] Tubular Labs: Shorts were 57% of uploads from U.S. creators and 88% of views in 2025 to date — [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/12/02/youtube-shorts-year-end-recap-mrbeast-squid-game-kpop-demon-hunters/amp/)

**TikTok**
- [NO OFFICIAL BENCHMARK] An ad-tech guide states there is no TikTok hook-rate benchmark with a published method and recommends comparing against your own account median; TikTok's hook metric uses a 2-second threshold, so it is not comparable to Meta's 3-second one — [Billo](https://billo.app/blog/hook-rate-to-hold-rate)
- [FOLKLORE / vendor] "70-85% retention in the first 3 seconds gets 2.2x more views" cites unnamed "2025 industry data" — [TTS Vibes](https://insights.ttsvibes.com/?p=533). Creator-run targets: 80%+ completion under 15 s, 60%+ for 15-30 s — [OpusClip](https://www.opus.pro/blog/tiktok-length-format-retention-data). A claimed 1.8M-video dataset (Q4 2025-Jan 2026): 7-second clips finish at 89.4% — [ttcalculator](https://ttcalculator.net/data/engagement/completion-rate-by-video-length/). None has visible methodology.
- [STUDY] Dash Social 2025 H2 TikTok benchmark (970 brand accounts): brand completion rates are low; recommends stronger hooks (specific figure not retrieved) — [Dash Social](https://www.dashsocial.com/blog/tiktok-benchmarks)
- [OFFICIAL] TikTok Next 2026 trend report (Jan 2026): lead theme "Irreplaceable Instinct"; signals "Reali-TEA", "Curiosity Detours", "Emotional ROI" — [TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/introducing-tiktok-next-2026-our-trend-forecast-for-marketers-for-the-year-ahead?lang=en), [PDF](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/TikTok_Next_2026_Trend_Report.pdf)

### Inferences
- For a ~30 s episode, the three platform objectives converge: (a) do not get swiped in the first 1-2 s, (b) hold to the end (a 30 s video at high average watch time beats a longer one that leaks), (c) make something a person would DM to one specific other person. Sends are the growth lever for reaching non-followers on Instagram, which is exactly a new series' situation.
- "Send-ability" for this product means recognition humor targeted at a role: the MEI owner sends it to a partner/accountant; the procurement analyst sends it to a colleague ("isso é a gente"). Write each episode with one named recipient in mind.
- 30 s is comfortably inside every platform's sweet spot; Buffer's finding that longer Reels reach slightly more does not argue for stretching a sketch, since the differences are a few percent and comedy depends on completion.
- Because half of Instagram viewing is muted, every joke must read without audio (see section 7).

### Gaps
- No primary Mosseri statement on a 3-second hold or view-rate metric was found; the task brief's "3-second hold" could not be confirmed as official.
- No direct Todd Beaupré quote on Shorts was retrieved; Creator Insider material was only available as paraphrase.
- TikTok Creator Academy / Creative Center guidance on hooks was not retrieved (the TikTok Next PT-BR PDF downloaded as unreadable binary).
- No audited completion-rate benchmark for 20-40 s comedy specifically.

## 2. How short-form sketches are structured

### Takeaway
Sources agree on one mechanical rule: establish who/what/where instantly using familiar types so the audience can start laughing, then run one comic idea through about four beats and end on the strongest joke. Exact "first laugh by second N" numbers for vertical video are not documented by credible sources.

### Cited Findings
- [PRACTITIONER] Chris Cray (animator/director, Somesense), "7 Top Tips for Animating Sketch Comedy" (Mar 2022): set the scene fast with familiar character types and tropes — "Only once the audience has figured out what's going on can they find it funny." — [Motionographer](https://motionographer.com/2022/03/03/7-top-tips-for-animating-sketch-comedy/)
- [PRACTITIONER] Same source: structure a joke as four beats like a four-panel comic strip ("Mapping out a joke into four beats is essential for creating a four-panel strip"); bold simplified line work suits phone screens the way it suits newspaper strips — [Motionographer](https://motionographer.com/2022/03/03/7-top-tips-for-animating-sketch-comedy/)
- [PRACTITIONER] Same source: keep camera and transitions simple; a jump cut can jolt, but give the viewer a moment to reorient — "If the audience is thinking, then they're not laughing." — [Motionographer](https://motionographer.com/2022/03/03/7-top-tips-for-animating-sketch-comedy/)
- [PRACTITIONER] General sketch-writing guides: a sketch explores one central comic idea rather than a plot; in a two-minute sketch aim for a laugh roughly every 15 seconds, with the strongest joke as the closing line — [Final Draft](https://www.finaldraft.com/blog/10-sketch-writing-tips-on-writing-from-sketch-masters), [Backstage](https://www.backstage.com/magazine/article/how-to-write-a-skit-77684/) (traditional sketch guidance, not vertical-specific)
- [PRACTITIONER] Internet-era sketch uses quick jump cuts, non sequiturs and low-budget production — [No Film School](https://nofilmschool.com/how-to-write-sketch-comedy)
- [PRACTITIONER, B2B] "Specificity drives the laugh": premises should come from real buyer experience; success shows up as comments like "painfully accurate" and peer tagging; a good skit communicates in about 30 seconds what a post needs about 900 words for — [Sweet Fish Media](https://www.sweetfishmedia.com/blog/b2b-video-skits-strategy) (May 2026)
- [PRACTITIONER] ClickUp writers room: everyone pitches on Monday, blind vote, winners are shot; about 10 videos/week; pushing volume higher reduced quality — [Muse by Clios](https://musebyclios.com/creators/how-clickup-became-the-snl-of-project-management-platforms/) (Jan 2025)
- [OFFICIAL feature relevant to endings] On Shorts every loop counts as another view — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-shorts-algorithm/)

### Inferences
- A 30 s episode maps cleanly to four beats: (1) 0-3 s cold open on the conflict with on-screen text naming the situation; (2) escalation; (3) second escalation or reversal; (4) punchline, then cut immediately. The traditional "laugh every 15 s" pace is far too slow for vertical; scaling it to a swipe feed implies a first laugh inside the first beat and roughly one laugh per beat (3-5 in 30 s). This is inference, not a sourced benchmark.
- Recurring characters are a structural shortcut: from episode 2 onward the who/where is already known, so the cold open can start directly on the conflict.
- Ending on the punchline with no outro and a last frame that cuts back plausibly to the first supports rewatches; loops inflate views on Shorts, though whether they are a ranking input is not stated officially.
- On-screen text formats ("POV:", "X tentando Y pela primeira vez") work as the instant who/what/where device Cray describes, and as the muted-viewer setup.

### Gaps
- No credible source giving "first laugh within N seconds" or optimal joke density for 20-40 s vertical comedy; no interviews with vertical sketch creators on structure were retrieved.
- No data comparing punchline endings vs. loop endings.
- No data on which text-hook formats perform best in Portuguese.

## 3. Recurring-character sketch creators (workplace/corporate and Brazilian)

### Takeaway
The proven format is one performer (or a tiny fixed cast) repeating a small set of recognisable workplace types, with humor based on validation ("this is my job") rather than jokes about strangers. In Brazil, Thaisa Valdez shows that CLT/PJ and workers'-rights confusion is itself strong comic material and attracts brands quickly.

### Cited Findings
- [PRACTITIONER] Corporate Natalie (Natalie Marshall): character built on office absurdities (passive-aggressive Slack messages, buzzword all-hands); over 2 million followers across platforms; first brand deal paid $500 (Twisted Tea); launched a creator-led agency (April 2026) — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/04/05/corporate-natalie-new-creator-led-agency-expand-co-lab-influencer-marketing/), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natalie_Marshall)
- [PRACTITIONER] Her partner filter: whether the product can be woven into content that feels natural for the character — [Marketing Brew](https://www.marketingbrew.com/stories/2023/03/23/what-corporate-natalie-looks-for-in-a-brand-partner) (Mar 2023). Paycom used her in a TV campaign because she credibly voiced frustration with outdated tech, tedious processes and HR mistakes — [Digiday](https://digiday.com/marketing/heres-why-paycom-partnered-with-tiktok-star-corporate-natalie-to-bring-digital-ads-to-tv/)
- [PRACTITIONER, 2022] CNBC profile of corporate-humor creators: Rod Thill (deadpan dark comedy about work stress; ex-sales), Laura Whaley (WFH and boundary/pay sketches that spark comment discussions; ex-IT consulting), DeAndre Brown (most popular clip: a 90-second skit of being caught slacking on a video call), Terrell Wade ("relatable content about disliking work", validating/anti-hustle) — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2022/03/01/why-corporate-humor-tiktoks-are-so-satisfying-people-are-sick-of-work.html) (Mar 1, 2022)
- [PRACTITIONER] ClickUp's two fixed actors are identified by costume: Luke the "blue shirt guy" and Adam who usually wears black; they satirise jargon and office silliness — [Muse by Clios](https://musebyclios.com/creators/how-clickup-became-the-snl-of-project-management-platforms/)
- [PRACTITIONER] tl;dv: employees post sketches as individuals, not from the company page; recurring character "Sam Altman's Mom" came "from my disdain for founder worship"; fast reaction to tech news — [Feed First](https://feedfirst.beehiiv.com/p/how-tl-dv-made-linkedin-feel-like-a-tv-show-with-ren-e-shaw) (Aug 2026)
- [BRAZIL] Thaisa Valdez: began recording office sketches in May 2023, first ad offer in July 2023; 50M+ Instagram views in seven months; followers from about 4,000 to 80,000, then about 120,000; brands: Nestlé, Cambly, Elma Chips, Buser — [Flash](https://flashapp.com.br/blog/thaisa-valdez-humor-corporativo-influencer) (Nov 2025, updated Jul 2026)
- [BRAZIL] Her best-performing themes: CLT vs PJ differences, remote work, flexible hours, escala 6x1, meetings, hiring discrimination. "Sempre que eu falo de CLT e PJ tem alguém perdido ali." She says humor makes hard topics easier to consume and work was long a taboo subject — [Flash](https://flashapp.com.br/blog/thaisa-valdez-humor-corporativo-influencer)
- [BRAZIL] Her "manifesto do trabalhador brasileiro" meme passed 2 million likes on Instagram in April 2025 — [Diário do Pará](https://diariodopara.com.br/entretenimento/quem-e-thaisa-valdez-influenciadora-por-tras-do-meme-seu-armando/)
- [BRAZIL] "CLTfluencers" trend: creators succeeding by showing day-to-day office life — [Na Prática](https://napratica.org.br/infuencers-clt/); parody of "teatro corporativo" and cringe HR/compliance training are recurring formats — [Fast Company Brasil](https://fastcompanybrasil.com/worklife/geracao-z-perde-a-paciencia-com-teatro-corporativo/), [Fast Company Brasil](https://fastcompanybrasil.com/worklife/tiktok-expoe-o-lado-mais-brega-dos-treinamentos-de-rh/)

### Inferences
- Pattern for the series: 2-3 fixed characters distinguishable by one visual marker (ClickUp's shirt colors), each embodying a type the audience already knows: the MEI owner who does everything, the procurement analyst buried in editais, the pregoeiro/órgão as the unseen antagonist.
- The engine is recognition plus mild grievance. Comment sections fill with people adding their own version, which matters because viewers' own stories extend watch time and sends.
- Thaisa Valdez's remark that people are always lost on CLT vs PJ suggests the same opening for licitação: confusion about the rules is both the joke and the reason to save or send the video.

### Gaps
- Nothing retrieved on "Corporate Erin" (Lisa Beasley); the CNBC article does not use the "Loewhaley" handle (it names Laura Whaley); no follower counts for Thill/Whaley.
- No source found on Brazilian creators doing MEI-specific or atendimento-specific sketch humor, nor on dedicated small-business humor creators.
- Typical video durations for these creators were not documented.

## 4. Animated short-form series and comedic timing in limited animation

### Takeaway
Credible, specific production detail on short-form animated series is thin; what is documented points to simple bold designs readable on a phone, legible faces, reusable rigs and minimal camera work, with timing (not motion quantity) carrying the comedy. Classic sources support this: Chuck Jones called animation the art of timing, and TV limited animation got by with mouths moving over static backgrounds.

### Cited Findings
- [PRACTITIONER] Faces must be big and detailed enough to read, because many jokes are a character's unexpected reaction: "it's crucial that the audience can read the expression on the character's face." — [Motionographer](https://motionographer.com/2022/03/03/7-top-tips-for-animating-sketch-comedy/)
- [PRACTITIONER] Use rigged puppets and reskin them (costumes, props) to produce many characters cheaply, switching to hand-drawn only for elaborate or nuanced moments: "a 2D sketch troupe." Only animate sketches that gain from being drawn ("Is there something about this sketch that would benefit from being drawn?") — [Motionographer](https://motionographer.com/2022/03/03/7-top-tips-for-animating-sketch-comedy/)
- [PRACTITIONER] Locked-off or subtle camera for dialogue comedy; add bumpers (title/end sequence) to turn sketches into episodes — [Motionographer](https://motionographer.com/2022/03/03/7-top-tips-for-animating-sketch-comedy/)
- [CRAFT] Chuck Jones: animation is the art of timing; Road Runner shorts are cited as master classes in timing. His internal rules included "No outside force can harm the coyote—only his own ineptitude or the failure of Acme products" and "The Coyote is always more humiliated than harmed by his failures." — [Wikipedia: Chuck Jones](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Chuck_Jones), [Senses of Cinema](https://www.sensesofcinema.com/2002/great-directors/jones/)
- [CRAFT] Jones used smear drawings for speed and sudden movement in The Dover Boys (1942); critics distinguish that deliberate stylisation from the standardised TV limited animation attributed to Hanna-Barbera, where "sometimes nothing moves but the mouths" over reused static backgrounds — [Senses of Cinema](https://www.sensesofcinema.com/2002/great-directors/jones/), [Time](https://content.time.com/time/magazine/article/0,9171,908340,00.html)
- [CRAFT] Timing and spacing are inseparable; animation on 1s and 2s is "full", lower drawing rates are "limited"; The Animator's Survival Kit covers timing/spacing, anticipation, takes and accents — [Full Frontal](https://fullfrontal.moe/animation-fundamentals-timing-and-spacing/), [Macmillan](https://us.macmillan.com/books/9780865478978/theanimatorssurvivalkit)
- [EXAMPLE] Chikn Nuggit: animated web series on TikTok, X, Instagram and later YouTube, created, directed, animated and voiced by Kyra Kupetsky; writers Kupetsky, Kevie Santos, Matt Pierce; developed by BuzzFeed Animation Lab with Frederator. A dispute ended March 11, 2026 with BuzzFeed agreeing not to use the characters in AI projects — [Wikipedia: Chikn Nuggit](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/articles/Chikn_Nuggit)
- [EXAMPLE] Haminations (Bryson): humorous story-time animation of relatable anecdotes; a marketing-style profile claims 7.4M+ YouTube subscribers (unverified) — [Favikon](https://favikon.com/blog/who-is-bryson-aka-haminations)
- [EXAMPLE] Knorr Brasil uses animated mascots Sukulenta and Saborosa on TikTok for humor and everyday tips — [Grandes Nomes da Propaganda](https://grandesnomesdapropaganda.com.br/mercado-digital/knorr-reforca-estrategia-digital-e-aposta-no-tiktok/)
- [CONTEXT] YouTube Culture & Trends: 61% of animation fans aged 14-24 prefer works from indie creators (April 2025 survey); Cartoon Brew notes there is no single "YouTube style"; dominant formats are memes, animatics and full episodes — [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/04/09/youtube-culture-trends-original-animated-series-digital-circus-hazbin-hotel/), [Cartoon Brew](https://www.cartoonbrew.com/distribution/youtube-animation-report-259897.html)
- [BRAZIL context] Sociedade da Virtude (dark comedy) is cited in a YouTube animation report; Irmão do Jorel and José Totoy are successful Brazilian animated IPs, though long-form — [YouTube report PDF](https://services.google.com/fh/files/blogs/yt-cats-report-a-nova-onda-da-animacao.pdf), [Meio & Mensagem](https://www.meioemensagem.com.br/midia/conheca-jose-totoy-a-animacao-brasileira-que-ganhou-o-mundo)
- [CAUTION] Lackadaisy is a Kickstarter-funded longer-form pilot (2023), not a Shorts-first series — [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/indie-animation-is-having-a-moment-on-youtube-2000745081)

### Inferences
- For a code-driven (Remotion) series, the affordable and well-precedented route is pose-to-pose limited animation: a small library of strong, readable poses and facial expressions per character, hard cuts between them, held frames for reactions, and mouth flaps rather than full lip-sync. The Hanna-Barbera precedent shows audiences accept mouths-only motion when writing and timing are good.
- Comedic timing in limited animation is mostly edit timing: the length of the hold before a reaction, the snap into a new pose, and a sound effect or music sting on the cut. A reaction shot held slightly too long is a classic laugh; that costs zero extra drawings.
- Chuck Jones's rules transfer directly as series bible discipline: fix what can and cannot happen (for example, the bureaucracy always wins unless the product is used; the hero is humiliated, never harmed), because consistent rules make recurring gags funnier.
- Frame the face large in 9:16: one or two characters, chest-up, so expression reads on a phone.

### Gaps
- No interviews or production breakdowns were found for Ketnipz, Haminations, Chikn Nuggit or Smiling Friends clips (style, frame rate, episode cadence), and no Brazilian short-form animated creator with documented numbers.
- No source quantifying the importance of lip-sync vs. mouth flaps, or of sound effects/stings, for retention.
- South Park's and Hanna-Barbera's specific timing methods and Richard Williams's specific passages on comedic holds were not retrieved; only general characterisations are cited.

## 5. Brand comedy: consumer brands, Brazil, and B2B/SaaS

### Takeaway
The brands that win treat the feed like a sitcom: a character with lore, most videos selling nothing, and a small minority of explicit product pieces (ClickUp: one or two out of about ten per week). Research from Kantar supports humor's effectiveness, with the condition that the humor be connected to the brand.

### Cited Findings
- [STUDY] Kantar: humor is "the most powerful creative enhancer of receptivity"; humorous ads are more expressive (+27 points), more involving (+14), more distinct (+11); share of ads using humor fell from 53% to 34% since 2000; only 33% of ads Kantar tests use humor yet half of its Creative Effectiveness Award winners do; "in order to be effective, humor must be connected to the brand" — [Kantar](https://www.kantar.com/inspiration/advertising-media/time-to-get-serious-about-humour-in-advertising), [Kantar](https://www.kantar.com/uki/inspiration/advertising-media/how-to-get-humour-right-in-advertising)
- [CASE] ClickUp: about 10 videos a week, one or two being ads for specific ClickUp services; low-budget, iPhone-shot; 150-180 million impressions per month; goal is awareness ("an additional billboard"), since buyers shop for project-management software only when ready — [Muse by Clios](https://musebyclios.com/creators/how-clickup-became-the-snl-of-project-management-platforms/) (Jan 2025). Comedy runs on a separate account from brand and product accounts; 500,000+ followers — [Content Ideas Substack](https://contentideas.substack.com/p/clickup-comedy), [Exit Five](https://exitfive.com/podcast/how-clickup-built-a-b2b-social-machine-with-chris-cunningham-from-clickup/)
- [CASE] Duolingo: Duo's feed is a fictional world with supporting characters (e.g. Lily); 16.8M TikTok followers (Apr 2025); CEO participates in bits (a Severance spoof filmed in 30 minutes) — [Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/brand-marketing/duolingo-duo-owl-marketing-strategy/). Growth from 50,000 to 16M+ followers — [The Drum](https://www.thedrum.com/news/2025/02/25/duolingo-s-tiktok-mastermind-its-unhinged-social-strategy-and-killing-its-mascot). "The comment section is our social brief."; spinoff series Living With Lily got 10.5M views and 100,000 new followers in its first run (as reported in search summary of Adweek) — [Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/brand-marketing/duolingo-duo-owl-marketing-strategy/)
- [CASE] Ryanair: low-budget, face-filter planes as recurring characters, self-deprecating, trend-led — [Socialinsider](https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/ryanair-social-media-strategy/), [Business Traveller](https://www.businesstraveller.com/insights/meme-machine-the-evil-genius-of-ryanairs-social-media-strategy/). Nutter Butter (absurdist anthropomorphic cookies) and Scrub Daddy (theatrical cleaning) — [NoGood](https://nogood.io/blog/unhinged-marketing/). No performance figures found for these three.
- [CAUTION] Fast Company has run pieces on "DGAF brands" and asking whether brain-rot brand TikTok has become cringe (titles only; content not retrieved) — [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91464170/is-brain-rot-tiktok-cringe?partner=rss)
- [B2B] tl;dv: comedy-first was the CEO's call; people message several times a week saying "I signed up because of the videos"; no numeric results published — [Feed First](https://feedfirst.beehiiv.com/p/how-tl-dv-made-linkedin-feel-like-a-tv-show-with-ren-e-shaw)

Kiso Insights: TikTok consumption in Brazil is intense but brand presence is still discreet — [mLabs](https://www.mlabs.com.br/?p=622680)

### Inferences
- Right dose of product: the product is the resolution of the conflict (or the contrast character who is not suffering), on screen for a few seconds at most, in a minority of episodes. Most episodes should be pure pain-recognition with only a consistent end card or character association. This follows ClickUp's ratio and Kantar's "humor must be connected to the brand": the connection comes from the subject matter (bids, editais), not from the logo.
- A dedicated comedy account or clearly named series (as ClickUp does) protects the product account's credibility with procurement professionals while letting the comedy be sharper.
- B2B comedy is an awareness play with soft attribution ("I signed up because of the videos"); plan measurement around sends, profile visits and branded search, not direct conversion.
- The federal government winning for meme explanations suggests Brazilian audiences accept humor about public administration from institutional voices, lowering the risk of joking about licitação.

### Gaps
- No Brazil-specific case data retrieved for Nubank, Netflix Brasil, Magalu's Lu, iFood, Ponto or Burger King Brasil on short video.
- No controlled evidence comparing entertainment-first vs. product-first branded short video performance; Kantar's data concerns ads generally. The Oracle "91% prefer brands to be funny" figure could not be verified.

## 6. The "Part 2" serialized format

### Takeaway
There is no data showing that cliffhanger "Part 2" videos help or hurt reach; what is documented is viewer frustration with withheld or unfindable sequels. The safe model is episodic (each episode a complete joke, shared world) rather than serial, with playlists and linked videos for navigation.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL feature] TikTok creator playlists let creators categorise public videos so viewers watch related posts in sequence; they can be reordered — [TikTok Support](https://support.tiktok.com/en/using-tiktok/creating-videos/creator-playlist). Availability reportedly varies by account (some creators above 10,000 followers still without it) — [Kapwing](https://kapwing.com/resources/tiktok-playlists)
- [OFFICIAL feature] YouTube lets creators attach a related video to a Short — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-shorts-algorithm/)
- [VIEWER REACTION] When TikTok tested 10-minute videos (2022), some users welcomed the end of "frustrating 'Like for part two' videos" that cut off at a crucial point — [Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/news/tiktok-begins-rolling-out-10-minute-videos-to-confusion-of-userbase)
- [VIEWER REACTION] A Boston University communications professor (Steph Yasmin) calls unfindable follow-ups "ghost results", describing the tactic as strategic but harmful; viewers searched for hours for a part 2 (surfaced via search; exact article URL not confirmed, likely) — [PressReader / Borneo Post, "Sorry you went viral"](https://www.pressreader.com/malaysia/the-borneo-post/20221128/282690461205390) (Nov 2022)
- [PRACTITIONER, low authority] Forum advice: Part 1 must work on its own, post parts on consecutive days, link Part 2 in a top comment on Part 1 — [Jeff Bullas forum](https://www.jeffbullas.com/thread/how-do-you-effectively-plan-and-launch-a-multi-part-video-series-on-tiktok/)
- [CASE] Serialized fiction can work at scale: the "Group Chat" TikTok series "captivated millions" (April 2025) — [IOL](https://iol.co.za/lifestyle/2025-04-07-the-group-chat-tiktok-series-has-captivated-millions-with-its-dramatic-storyline/); Duolingo's Living With Lily spinoff (10.5M views, 100,000 new followers) — [Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/brand-marketing/duolingo-duo-owl-marketing-strategy/)

### Inferences
- Recommendations work per video and most viewers arrive mid-series from the feed, so each episode must deliver a full setup and punchline. Continuity should be optional reward (running gags, callbacks, "episódio 7" labels) rather than a requirement.
- If a two-parter is used, give Part 1 its own laugh-line ending, publish Part 2 within a day, pin a comment linking it, and add both to a playlist. Unfindable sequels are the documented source of resentment.
- Numbered episode labels and a named series build the habit and profile-visit loop that Duolingo's "lore" exploits, without the cliffhanger penalty.

### Gaps
- No platform statement or dataset on whether "Part 2" titles affect distribution, and no retention data for serialized vs. standalone short comedy.
- Nothing retrieved on Instagram's series/linking features for Reels, or on microdrama retention benchmarks.

## 7. Captions, sound design, trending audio and the first frame

### Takeaway
Design for sound-off first: Mosseri says about half of Instagram video is watched muted, and older survey data puts mobile muted viewing even higher. Captions and a self-explanatory first frame are therefore part of the joke delivery, while audio (voices, stings, effects) is the bonus layer for the other half.

### Cited Findings
- [OFFICIAL, via Buffer] Mosseri: about half of Instagram videos are watched without sound; audio can still attract people who browse with sound on — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/instagram-algorithms/)
- [STUDY, older and sponsored] Sharethrough research in a sponsored Digiday piece (circa 2020-2021): 75% of people watch mobile video on mute; 85% of Millennials vs 64% of Gen X often keep the phone muted; captions on video ads raised comprehension by 56% (test of 750 people) — [Digiday](https://digiday.com/sponsored/75-percent-of-people-watch-mobile-videos-on-mute/)
- [OFFICIAL, via Buffer] Reels with other apps' watermarks/logos are demoted, so export clean masters per platform — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/instagram-algorithms/)
- [PRACTITIONER] Viewers need the who/what/where immediately; faces must be legible; after a cut the viewer needs a moment to reorient — [Motionographer](https://motionographer.com/2022/03/03/7-top-tips-for-animating-sketch-comedy/)
- [OFFICIAL] On Shorts a view now counts when the Short starts to play, so the first frame is what determines "viewed vs. swiped away" — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-shorts-algorithm/)
- [CASE] Ryanair built its strategy around trending sounds and a face filter — [Business Traveller](https://www.businesstraveller.com/insights/meme-machine-the-evil-genius-of-ryanairs-social-media-strategy/)

### Inferences
- First frame checklist for an animated sketch: character already mid-emotion (no fade-in, no logo, no title card), a text hook of about 5-8 words naming the situation, high contrast, face large in frame. A title card costs the first second, which is when the swipe decision happens.
- Burn in captions timed to delivery, revealing the punchline word on the beat rather than ahead of it. Captions that show the whole line early kill the timing for muted viewers.
- Original dialogue audio makes the series its own "sound" and supports character identity; trending audio suits one-off trend episodes but is harder for a dialogue-driven animated sketch. Sound effects and music stings should land on cuts to sharpen timing for sound-on viewers.

### Gaps
- No 2025-2026 platform data on muted viewing for TikTok or Shorts (TikTok is generally described as sound-on, but no figure was found).
- No data on trending vs. original audio performance for comedy, and no measured effect of captions on comedy retention specifically.

## 8. Bureaucracy, MEI and public-bid themes in Brazilian short-form humor

### Takeaway
Workplace and labour-regime humor (CLT/PJ, 6x1, meetings, HR training) is well established and performs strongly in Brazil, and Sebrae already uses humor for MEI routine; no humor content specifically about licitação, edital or pregão was found. The niche looks open, with real audience pain documented by Sebrae research.

### Cited Findings
- [PERFORMANCE] Thaisa Valdez's sketches on CLT vs PJ, 6x1, remote work and hiring bias: 50M+ Instagram views in seven months; "manifesto do trabalhador brasileiro" meme with 2M+ likes (April 2025) — [Flash](https://flashapp.com.br/blog/thaisa-valdez-humor-corporativo-influencer), [Diário do Pará](https://diariodopara.com.br/entretenimento/quem-e-thaisa-valdez-influenciadora-por-tras-do-meme-seu-armando/)
- [TREND] Parodies of corporate theatre and of HR/compliance training circulate widely on Brazilian TikTok — [Fast Company Brasil](https://fastcompanybrasil.com/worklife/geracao-z-perde-a-paciencia-com-teatro-corporativo/), [Fast Company Brasil](https://fastcompanybrasil.com/worklife/tiktok-expoe-o-lado-mais-brega-dos-treinamentos-de-rh/)
- [MEI] Sebrae's humorous Semana do MEI films (Evelyn Castro, Estevam Nabote) dramatise "adversidades e a rotina real" of solo entrepreneurs; "MEI Minuto" delivers information in 30 seconds with humor — [Marcas pelo Mundo](https://marcaspelomundo.com.br/anunciantes/sebrae-anuncia-semana-do-mei-em-serie-de-filmes-bem-humorados/), [Agência Sebrae](https://agenciasebrae.com.br/?p=30666)
- [PAIN] Sebrae research: bureaucratic intricacies of formalisation stall business opening; the idea that formalising means "papéis, documentos e formulários" fuels fear of bureaucracy (2023 qualitative study). A Sebrae PR "Sondagem Econômica MEI – Burocracia e Obrigações" exists (Aug 2026; content not retrieved) — [DataSebrae](https://datasebrae.com.br/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/SEBRAE-Quali-FORMALIZACAO-v6.pdf), [Sebrae PR](https://sebraepr.com.br/?p=547223)
- [PUBLIC SECTOR] Brazilian public institutions use memes/humor to communicate, with academic caution about appropriateness (FGV, Revista de Administração Pública); the federal government's TikTok account was recognised at TikTok Ad Awards 2025 for meme explanations — [FGV RAP](https://periodicos.fgv.br/rap/article/download/98097/90925/228691), [Meio & Mensagem](https://www.meioemensagem.com.br/midia/sowhat-e-mercado-livre-vencem-tiktok-ad-awards-2025)
- [VOCABULARY] Sebrae's own MEI bidding material supplies ready-made comic premises: "o edital é a lei da licitação"; the pregoeiro conducts the whole pregão — [Sebrae PA](https://sebrae.com.br/Sebrae/Portal%20Sebrae/UFs/PA/Anexos/SEMANA%20DO%20MEI%202023/MEI_Licitacao_1.pdf)
- [NOT FOUND] A targeted search for licitação / pregoeiro / edital humor on TikTok and Reels returned only official edital PDFs and training material, no creators or viral examples.

### Inferences
- Licitação humor appears to be unoccupied territory in short video, which is an opportunity (ownable niche, high in-group recognition among analysts and licitantes) and a risk (general audiences lack the vocabulary). Episodes should anchor on universally understood bureaucracy pain (a 90-page PDF, a missing certidão, a deadline at 23:59, a portal that crashes) and use bid jargon as flavor.
- Two audiences, two registers: MEI/ME episodes can borrow the proven "pequeno empreendedor faz tudo sozinho" frame (Sebrae precedent); analyst episodes can borrow corporate-humor frames (Thaisa Valdez, ClickUp) transplanted to the bid desk.
- Target the system (edital length, portals, deadlines), not public servants as people, consistent with the FGV caution and with Chuck Jones's "humiliated, never harmed" principle, since pregoeiros and órgãos are part of the product's ecosystem.

### Gaps
- No evidence of which bureaucracy or licitação topics "performed well", because no such content was found; this needs direct in-app searches (TikTok/Instagram hashtags such as #licitação, #pregão, #MEI) that web search cannot replicate.
- No view data for Sebrae's humorous MEI series, and no Porta dos Fundos or similar bureaucracy-sketch performance figures retrieved.
